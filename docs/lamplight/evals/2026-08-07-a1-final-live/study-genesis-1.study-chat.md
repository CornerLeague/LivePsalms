## Reply

The refrain “God saw that it was good” functions as God’s own assessment of creation. “Good” here is not merely attractive or morally harmless; within the chapter, it means that what God has made is fitting, ordered, fruitful, and suited to its appointed purpose. Light is distinguished from darkness, seas from land, and living things reproduce “according to their kinds” (Genesis 1:4, 10, 12, 18, 21, 25).

The phrase also marks stages in a movement from an unformed, unfilled earth toward an inhabited world. Genesis begins with the earth “formless and void” (Genesis 1:2). God then gives it structure and fills its realms with life. This accords with Isaiah’s description of the Lord as the One who “did not create it to be empty, but formed it to be inhabited” (Isaiah 45:18). The repeated verdict therefore tells us that creation is neither an accident nor a rival divine power: it is the deliberate work of God’s word, answering to His will (Genesis 1:3; Revelation 4:11).

The repetition prepares for the climactic judgment after humanity is created: “indeed, it was very good” (Genesis 1:31). Humanity does not make an otherwise defective world good. Rather, male and female are placed within an already good creation as God’s image-bearers, entrusted with rule that reflects His ordering care (Genesis 1:26–28).

Finally, the refrain establishes the Bible’s baseline for understanding the world. Before Genesis narrates human sin, it insists that created existence as God gave it is good. The material world is therefore not presented as evil in itself. Its deepest meaning is received from its Creator, and its proper response is worship: “You created all things; by Your will they exist and were created” (Revelation 4:11).
