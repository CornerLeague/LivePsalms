## Reply

Romans 9 certainly teaches that God’s saving purpose rests on his call and mercy rather than human ancestry, achievement, desire, or effort. Paul points to Jacob and Esau: before they “had done anything good or bad,” God distinguished between them so that his purpose would stand “not by works but by Him who calls” (Romans 9:10–16). The chapter therefore gives divine initiative unmistakable priority.

Whether this specifically means that God eternally chooses particular individuals for final salvation is disputed among orthodox Christians.

**Reformed readings** generally answer yes. They understand Jacob and Esau as illustrating unconditional election of individuals, then connect this with the potter’s authority and the “vessels of mercy” whom God “prepared in advance for glory” (Romans 9:18–24). On this reading, mercy is not God’s response to foreseen faith; faith itself belongs within his gracious calling.

**Wesleyan/Arminian readings** emphasize that Paul’s immediate question concerns God’s covenant purpose through Israel and the inclusion of Gentiles. Jacob and Esau represent not only persons but covenant lines and peoples; Paul’s citation, “Jacob I loved,” comes from a passage addressing Israel and Edom (Romans 9:10–13; Malachi 1:2). The chapter’s conclusion also identifies the decisive contrast as faith versus works: Gentiles received righteousness by faith, while Israel stumbled by pursuing it as though it came through works (Romans 9:30–33). On this reading, God sovereignly determines the saving way and covenant community without the passage necessarily describing an irrevocable selection of each individual before birth.

Paul’s central claim is clearer than the disputed mechanism: God’s word has not failed, belonging to Abraham’s physical family cannot secure salvation, and nobody can place God in their debt. Salvation rests on the Lord’s mercy and is received through faith in Christ, the cornerstone (Romans 9:6–16, 30–33; Isaiah 28:16). Since faithful churches differ on how individual election operates here, this is worth studying with your pastor or church community rather than treating either reading as self-evident.
