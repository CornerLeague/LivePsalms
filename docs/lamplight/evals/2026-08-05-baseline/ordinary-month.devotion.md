# ordinary-month · devotion

_An unremarkable month. The hardest case: nothing dramatic to hang a devotion on._

Sam — A morning run, a properly made meal, light held briefly on a wall, and a slower return to a book: attention is finding its boundaries.

psa 16:6

The lines of my boundary have fallen in pleasant places; surely my inheritance is delightful.

The image in psa 16:6 is not of possessing everything, but of receiving a marked-out portion and discovering delight within it. Your notes keep returning to ordinary limits: one morning’s run, work that was fine, a meal prepared, a minute before light, a book begun again more slowly. Read against them, the verse makes a different kind of abundance visible. The boundary lines are not merely restrictions; they can become the place where an inheritance is noticed. The slower reading especially belongs here: delight is not seized by rushing past what is given, but recognized as attention rests within it. Scripture holds the small and the sufficient together.

Where have the edges of an ordinary day become a place of delight?
