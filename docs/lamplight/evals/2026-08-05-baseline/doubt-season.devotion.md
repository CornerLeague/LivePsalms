# doubt-season · devotion

_Sustained honest doubt. Tests that it is not resolved on the reader's behalf._

Tom — beneath the words you keep singing and the prayers that seem to meet only plaster, a harder question persists: why does absence hurt if longing has nowhere to go?

psa 13:1

For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever? How long will You hide Your face from me?

Psalm 13 begins not by resolving silence but by giving it words fit for worship: “How long, O LORD?” The heading places this cry in Israel’s sung prayer, so David’s sense of being forgotten is not excluded from prayer; it becomes its address. Read against your notes, the passage holds the same tension: you speak toward God while unsure anyone hears, and you sing while belief feels fractured. The psalm does not call that tension hypocrisy. Its repeated “How long” names a relationship strained by hiddenness, not a question treated as trivial. Your longing and your protest are already turned toward the One whose face seems hidden (psa 13:1).

What changes in the question of God’s silence when you let it remain addressed to the LORD?
