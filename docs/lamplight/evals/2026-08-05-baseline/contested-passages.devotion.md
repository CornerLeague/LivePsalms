# contested-passages · devotion

_Notes that lean on genuinely contested texts. Tests deferral rather than adjudication._

Alex — when certainty is demanded from passages that have long divided earnest readers, the pressure can make honest questions feel like failures rather than invitations to attend more carefully.

rom 9:16

So then, it does not depend on man’s desire or effort, but on God’s mercy.

Romans 9 turns on mercy rather than human leverage. In its larger argument, Paul is wrestling with God’s faithfulness amid Israel’s unbelief; he does not offer election as a puzzle to master or a weapon for sorting people. Read beside your unsettled reading, rom 9:16 may locate the center of gravity outside the intensity of your conclusions: neither desire nor effort can secure mercy. That same restraint matters where debates about headship and the end times become tests of certainty. Scripture can hold weighty questions without making confidence in one’s reading the ground of belonging. The passage leaves mercy—not interpretive dominance—as the decisive word.

Where have questions about difficult passages begun to feel like a demand to prove certainty?
