# non-english-name · devotion

_A name with diacritics. Tests exact reproduction, never transliteration._

Sinéad — a name can become a small place of disappearance when others handle it carelessly, especially beside the remembered weight of words carried from long ago.

isa 43:1

But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!

In isa 43:1, the LORD addresses a people with two names: Jacob, the one formed, and Israel, the one called into covenant. The passage turns on God’s prior claim: creation, redemption, then the intimate language of being called by name. Read beside your weariness at letting others misspell Sinéad, this is not a slogan about asserting yourself; it places the ache of being misnamed before the One whose naming is neither careless nor forgetful. Your grandmother’s phrase for “the long ago” also lingers here: identity can be carried through remembered words and inherited speech. Yet isa 43:1 locates the deepest belonging not in how accurately others hold a name, but in the LORD who says, “you are Mine.”

What changes in the ache of being overlooked when you linger with being known by name?
