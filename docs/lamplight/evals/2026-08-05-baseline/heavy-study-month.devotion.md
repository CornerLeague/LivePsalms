Priya — The psalm’s final word does not erase its longing for home; it gives longing a posture when the door has not yet opened.

psa 27:14

Wait patiently for the LORD; be strong and courageous. Wait patiently for the LORD!

The abrupt turn you noticed—from confidence to pleading—makes the ending weightier. psa 27:14 does not close with arrival in the house sought earlier; it repeats “Wait patiently,” as though faith must speak again when resolution has not appeared. The passage turns on the LORD as the object of waiting, not merely the hoped-for change. Read beside your attention to “dwell,” this waiting need not mean a visit to God postponed until circumstances settle. The psalm holds desire and delay together: courage is named in the same breath as remaining turned toward the Lord. Its final repetition leaves the prayer open, yet not empty.

What changes when waiting is understood as remaining oriented toward the LORD rather than merely postponement?
