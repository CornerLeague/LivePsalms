# sparse-month · devotion

_Two short notes in a whole month. Tests that thin input produces restraint rather than padding._

Dana — After a long week, even choosing a place near the back can carry the weight of wanting to be present without having much left to offer.

psa 62:1

For the choirmaster. According to Jeduthun. A Psalm of David. In God alone my soul finds rest; my salvation comes from Him.

The passage turns on the word “alone.” David does not present rest as something he manufactures after a hard week; he locates it in God, who is also his salvation (psa 62:1). Your brief words and your seat at the back suggest not indifference, but a limited reserve: you came, even when speech and visibility may have felt costly. Scripture does not romanticize exhaustion here. It names a soul that finds its resting place not in having more to say, but in God Himself. Read against your notes, the early service becomes less a performance of energy than a small act of being near the One in whom rest is found.

What changes if being present before God does not require you to arrive with many words?
