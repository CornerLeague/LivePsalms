# non-english-name · devotion

_A name with diacritics. Tests exact reproduction, never transliteration._

Sinéad — when a name is repeatedly loosened from its true shape, even a small correction can begin to feel like too much to carry.

Isaiah 43:1

But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!

Isaiah 43:1 speaks to Jacob and Israel as a people with a long, wounded history; the passage turns not on their ability to hold themselves together, but on the LORD who created, formed, and redeemed them. Against the weariness in your writing about being misnamed, “I have called you by your name” gives particularity weight. It is not merely that God knows a category or a public version of a person. The Creator addresses those He has made. Your thought about the long ago also sits near this: the God who names is not forgetful of the history carried in a name. Scripture holds belonging and remembrance together in Isaiah 43:1.

What feels bound up in being known by your name, rather than a version others find easier?
