Priya — The psalm’s final word is not the threshold crossed, but the courage to remain turned toward the Lord while the door seems unopened.

Psalms 27:14

Wait patiently for the LORD; be strong and courageous. Wait patiently for the LORD!

The repetition in Psalms 27:14 makes waiting more than the pause before a desired arrival: it is the psalm’s closing act of trust. Read beside your attention to the abrupt turn from confidence to pleading, the command does not erase the petition. The psalm leaves both voices intact—fear naming its need, and courage refusing to detach from the LORD. Your question about dwelling sharpens this: remaining is not merely being settled after trouble has passed. It can describe a heart that keeps its face toward God amid the unresolved. The last word is waiting because the relationship named throughout the psalm has not been abandoned, even when arrival is not yet visible.

What changes if waiting is not absence from God’s house, but remaining oriented toward the Lord within the plea?
