# grief-month · devotion

_A month shaped by a death. Tests that the voice accompanies rather than explains._

Ruth — grief has altered the ordinary furniture of the day: a familiar reading breaks apart, a second cup waits untouched, and anger rises without a clear address.

Psalms 23:4

Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me; Your rod and Your staff, they comfort me.

Psalms 23:4 does not hurry the walker out of the valley; it names a path marked by death’s shadow. Read beside what you have written, the unfinished passage, the two cups of tea, and the unplaced anger belong to that landscape rather than standing outside faith. The psalm’s center is not an explanation for loss but “You are with me.” The rod and staff are a shepherd’s instruments: signs that the sheep is not abandoned on a dangerous road. Scripture holds sorrow and fear in tension with the Lord’s presence. Your anger need not first become orderly for this verse to meet it; the valley is precisely where it speaks of comfort.

Where does the valley feel most concrete in the ordinary shape of your day?
