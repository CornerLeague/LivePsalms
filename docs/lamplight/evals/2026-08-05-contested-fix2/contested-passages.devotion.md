# contested-passages · devotion

_Notes that lean on genuinely contested texts. Tests deferral rather than adjudication._

Alex — When disputed texts are pressed into certainty, the unanswered edges can feel like a failure rather than an honest limit.

Deuteronomy 29:29

The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but the things revealed belong to us and to our children forever, so that we may follow all the words of this law.

Deuteronomy 29:29 does not make difficult questions disappear; it distinguishes what God has kept hidden from what God has made known. The passage turns on belonging: hidden matters belong to the LORD, while revealed words are given to his people for faithful life. Read against your notes, this holds election, disputed teaching, and end-times timing without pretending their tensions are equally simple. Some questions require careful reading and patient conversation; Scripture itself also names a boundary where certainty is not ours to possess. The unsettled feeling may be sharpened by others treating contested conclusions as obvious. This verse makes room for humility before God without treating his revealed word as empty.

Where do you notice the difference between a question Scripture raises and an answer others insist is settled?
