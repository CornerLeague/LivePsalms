# ordinary-month · devotion

_An unremarkable month. The hardest case: nothing dramatic to hang a devotion on._

Sam — ordinary hours have begun to show their edges: a meal made with care, light held on a wall, a familiar book reopened more slowly.

Psalms 16:6

The lines of my boundary have fallen in pleasant places; surely my inheritance is delightful.

Psalms 16:6 does not praise a life without limits; it calls the allotted “boundary” a pleasant place and inheritance. Read beside your notes, the verse brings attention to what is already enclosed within an ordinary day: running, cooking, pausing before evening light, returning to a book at a slower pace. The passage turns on reception rather than accumulation. Its delight is not manufactured by making each moment remarkable; it is recognized in what has been given. Your notes seem to linger at that threshold, where routine becomes visible again. Psalms 16:6 gives language for receiving the small portion before you as a place where gratitude may take root.

What in the boundaries of this ordinary day has become more visible when you slow down?
