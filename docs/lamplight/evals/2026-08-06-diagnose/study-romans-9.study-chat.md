## Reply

Romans 9 certainly teaches that God’s saving purpose rests on his call and mercy, not on human ancestry, works, desire, or effort. Paul points to Jacob and Esau before their birth: the choice occurred “before the twins were born or had done anything good or bad,” so that God’s purpose would stand “not by works but by Him who calls” (rom 9:11–16).

Whether this specifically means God unconditionally chooses each individual for eternal salvation before birth is disputed among orthodox Christians. One reading sees Jacob and Esau as examples of individual election to salvation. Another notes that Paul draws from Israel’s corporate history: Jacob and Esau represent covenant peoples, and the chapter’s stated problem is Israel’s place in God’s promise (rom 9:4–8). The later emphasis is on Jews and Gentiles being called as peoples, with righteousness received by faith (rom 9:24–33). Malachi’s original setting also contrasts Jacob/Israel with Esau/Edom; Jamieson nevertheless reads that choice as grounded in God’s gratuitous favor (mal 1:2).

So the safest conclusion from this chapter alone is: God’s election precedes and excludes human merit, and salvation depends upon divine mercy.
