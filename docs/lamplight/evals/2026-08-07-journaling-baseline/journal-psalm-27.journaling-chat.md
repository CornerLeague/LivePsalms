## Reply

Psalm 27 does not present bravery as a feeling David has mastered. The psalm moves from “whom shall I fear?” to “hear… be merciful and answer me,” and even fears being forsaken. Its confidence rests not in an untroubled inner life but in who the LORD is: “my light,” “my salvation,” and “the stronghold of my life.” Read beside your sleepless calculations and the words you could not sing, that matters: the psalm makes room for a voice that is afraid and still turns toward God. “Wait… be strong and courageous” comes at the end of a prayer that has already named danger—not as denial, but as endurance anchored in the LORD’s goodness in “the land of the living.”
