## Reply

Psalm 27 does not present courage as a feeling David first achieves. It moves from “whom shall I fear?” to “Hear, O LORD… be merciful and answer me,” and even “do not leave me” (vv. 1, 7, 9). The confidence and the cry occupy the same prayer.

Read beside your sleepless calculations and the words that would not come in church, the psalm makes room for fear without making fear the final truth. David calls the LORD his stronghold while surrounded by threat; he then waits for the LORD rather than pretending danger is absent. “Be strong and courageous” is framed by that waiting, not by emotional certainty (v. 14). Isaiah likewise grounds fear’s answer in God’s presence and help, not in the person’s ability to feel brave (Isa. 41:10).
