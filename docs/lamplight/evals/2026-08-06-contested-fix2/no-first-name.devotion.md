Lamentations 3:23

They are new every morning; great is Your faithfulness!

Lamentations places “every morning” beside God’s faithfulness, not beside an easy beginning. Read against your notes, the passage makes room for a morning marked by poor sleep and prayer for neighbours at once. The newness named here is not the denial of weariness; it is the steady reality that God’s mercy is not exhausted when a person wakes depleted. Your later note notices that the day became gentler than it first appeared. That movement does not make the hard start unreal. It simply keeps the first hour from becoming the final interpretation of the day. “Great is Your faithfulness!” (Lamentations 3:23) gives the change its deepest ground: the Lord’s constancy, rather than the day’s predictability.

What changes when the first hours are received as incomplete rather than final?
