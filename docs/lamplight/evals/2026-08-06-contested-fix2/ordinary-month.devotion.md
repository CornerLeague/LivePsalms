# ordinary-month · devotion

_An unremarkable month. The hardest case: nothing dramatic to hang a devotion on._

Sam — The ordinary parts of a day can become a kind of boundary line: a meal made, a wall lit by evening, a page approached more slowly.

Psalms 16:6

The lines of my boundary have fallen in pleasant places; surely my inheritance is delightful.

Psalms 16:6 does not praise a life without limits; it calls the boundary itself “pleasant.” The image is of an allotted portion, received rather than seized. Read against your notes, the passage brings attention to the small spaces already given: a morning run, food prepared, light held on a kitchen wall, and a book reopened without hurry. None is dramatic, yet each belongs to the portion before you. The psalm’s delight is not manufactured by making every moment special. It rises from recognizing that a bounded life can contain gift. Your slower return to reading seems to inhabit that truth: there is room to receive what was there before.

Where have the boundaries of this ordinary day held more gift than you first noticed?
