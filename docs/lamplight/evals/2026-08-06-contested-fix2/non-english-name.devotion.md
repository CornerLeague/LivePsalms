# non-english-name · devotion

_A name with diacritics. Tests exact reproduction, never transliteration._

Sinéad — When a name is repeatedly handled carelessly, something deeper can begin to feel misplaced: not merely a spelling, but the sense of being truly seen.

Isaiah 43:1

But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!

Isaiah 43:1 addresses Jacob and Israel as a people marked by a long history, yet the LORD speaks of them with intimate particularity: created, formed, redeemed, and called by name. The passage turns on belonging before fear; “you are Mine” is not a reward for being recognized rightly by others, but the Creator’s claim on those He has formed. Read beside your weariness over correcting your name, this can illuminate why the small act carries weight. A name is not incidental when it bears a person’s particular identity. Your thought of the long ago also sits nearby: the God who addresses His people by name is not distant from the histories and inheritances that have shaped them.

What feels bound up with your name that a casual misspelling cannot quite touch?
