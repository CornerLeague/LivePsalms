# grief-month · devotion

_A month shaped by a death. Tests that the voice accompanies rather than explains._

Ruth — grief has altered the ordinary rituals: a second cup waits, and anger rises where words once came easily.

Psalms 34:18

The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite in spirit.

Psalms 34:18 does not explain the loss or rush the house back into fullness. It names where the LORD places Himself: near the brokenhearted. Read beside the unfinished reading at the service and the tea made for two, that nearness meets grief not only in tears but in disrupted habit. The passage also holds room for the anger with no clear target. A contrite spirit is not polished composure; it is a spirit brought low by what it cannot master. The LORD’s saving in Psalms 34:18 is therefore not presented as denial of sorrow, but as His presence with those whose inner life has been broken open.

Where does the absence feel most concrete today, and what does Psalms 34:18 name there?
