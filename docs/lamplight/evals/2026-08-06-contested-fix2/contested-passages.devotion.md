Alex — Some questions resist being made small: they expose the strain between wanting a clear answer and recognizing that Scripture itself leaves room for reverent restraint.

Psalms 131:1

A song of ascents. Of David. My heart is not proud, O LORD, my eyes are not haughty. I do not aspire to great things or matters too lofty for me.

Psalms 131:1 is not a celebration of incuriosity. David brings his heart and eyes before the LORD, naming the posture with which he approaches matters beyond his grasp. Read against your notes, the passage turns on a distinction: difficult questions about election, headship, and the end are not made trivial, yet neither are they made a contest in which certainty proves maturity. “Matters too lofty” may include truths Scripture reveals truly without resolving every question we bring to them. The friction in your group and the unease you felt in Romans 9 both show how quickly doctrine can become a measure of control. Psalms 131:1 holds intellectual seriousness together with humility before the LORD.

Where do you notice the desire for certainty becoming heavier than the truth presently given?
