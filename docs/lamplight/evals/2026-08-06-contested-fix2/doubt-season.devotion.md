# doubt-season · devotion

_Sustained honest doubt. Tests that it is not resolved on the reader's behalf._

Tom — the hardest prayers may be the ones that leave the room unchanged, while longing refuses to leave with them.

Psalms 13:1

For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever? How long will You hide Your face from me?

Psalms 13:1 does not tidy the experience of praying into an answer. David addresses the LORD precisely by asking whether he has been forgotten and why God’s face seems hidden. The passage turns on that tension: the cry of abandonment is still directed toward God, not away from him. Read against your notes, the words you sang and the prayer that seemed to meet only a ceiling may not resolve the question of belief; they do show that the question is being carried into God’s presence. Your argument names two silences—the apparent silence of God and the persistence of longing. Psalms 13:1 gives biblical language for the first without pretending it is small or shameful.

What changes when the question “How long?” is treated as a prayer rather than a failure of faith?
