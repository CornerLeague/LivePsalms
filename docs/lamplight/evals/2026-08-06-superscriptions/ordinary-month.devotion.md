# ordinary-month · devotion

_An unremarkable month. The hardest case: nothing dramatic to hang a devotion on._

Sam — ordinary care, a passing wash of evening light, and a slower return to a book suggest attention widening around the life already in your hands.

Psalms 16:6

The lines of my boundary have fallen in pleasant places; surely my inheritance is delightful.

Psalms 16:6 does not make delight dependent on a larger life elsewhere; it speaks of received boundaries and an inheritance recognized as good. Read beside your notes, the passage turns on noticing: a meal prepared, light held on a kitchen wall, pages revisited without haste. None of these is spectacular, yet each belongs within the portion you have been given. The slower beginning of the book especially echoes the verse’s movement from possession to perception: inheritance becomes “delightful” as it is received rather than rushed past. Scripture holds contentment here not as denial of longing, but as clear-eyed gratitude for what is present within the lines of a day.

What in the boundaries of this day has become easier to see when you slow down?
