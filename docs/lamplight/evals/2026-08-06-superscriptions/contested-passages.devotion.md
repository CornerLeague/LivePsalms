# contested-passages · devotion

_Notes that lean on genuinely contested texts. Tests deferral rather than adjudication._

Alex — Some questions become strained when a group treats mystery as a defect, rather than noticing where Scripture itself draws a boundary around what has been disclosed.

Deuteronomy 29:29

The secret things belong to the LORD our God, but the things revealed belong to us and to our children forever, so that we may follow all the words of this law.

Deuteronomy 29:29 does not make difficult questions unreal; it distinguishes between what God has kept in his own counsel and what he has made known. That distinction matters where election has left you unsettled, where a discussion of women teaching became painful, and where confidence about tribulation timing has exceeded clarity. The passage turns on belonging: the “secret things” belong to the LORD, while the revealed things are given to God’s people. Read against your notes, this can expose a pressure to make every debated matter feel equally settled. Scripture makes room for intellectual humility without treating revealed truth as empty or optional.

Where do you feel pressure to sound certain when Scripture has not spoken with the same degree of clarity?
