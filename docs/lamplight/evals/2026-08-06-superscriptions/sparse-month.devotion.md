# sparse-month · devotion

_Two short notes in a whole month. Tests that thin input produces restraint rather than padding._

Dana — Some weeks leave only a few words behind, and even a place near the back can carry more weariness than it shows.

Matthew 11:28

Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.

Jesus’ invitation in Matthew 11:28 begins not with an account of why the burden is there, but with the burdened person coming to him. Read beside your sparse words and your place at the early service, the passage makes room for a faith that arrives tired rather than articulate. “All you who are weary” does not require a polished report of the week. The rest Jesus names is his gift, not a reward for having explained oneself well. Your notes seem to hold a small but real movement toward him: not much to say, yet present. Scripture lets both realities stand together—weariness honestly named and Christ’s welcome held out.

What does it mean to be present before Jesus when you have little language for the week?
