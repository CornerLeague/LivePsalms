# grief-month · devotion

_A month shaped by a death. Tests that the voice accompanies rather than explains._

Ruth — grief has entered the ordinary rituals of the house, while anger moves through the loss without yet finding a name.

Psalms 34:18

The LORD is near to the brokenhearted; He saves the contrite in spirit.

Psalms 34:18 does not make brokenheartedness a failure of faith or ask it to become orderly. It names the place where the LORD draws near. Read beside the unfinished reading at the service and the extra cup of tea, the verse gives dignity to grief’s interruptions: love has been severed, and the body keeps reaching for the person who is gone. The anger in your note may belong to this same rupture—not necessarily aimed at someone, but arising where death has torn through what was cherished. The passage holds nearness and devastation together. “Brokenhearted” is not a condition from which one must first escape to be met by the LORD; it is the very place the verse says He approaches.

Where does loss most interrupt the ordinary shape of your day?
