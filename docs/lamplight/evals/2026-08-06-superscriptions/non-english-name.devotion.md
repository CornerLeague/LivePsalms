# non-english-name · devotion

_A name with diacritics. Tests exact reproduction, never transliteration._

Sinéad — When a name is repeatedly mishandled, even a small correction can begin to feel like asking for too much room.

Isaiah 43:1

But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!

Isaiah 43:1 addresses Jacob and Israel as a people marked by fear and displacement, yet the passage begins not with their response but with the LORD as Creator and Former. The words “called you by your name” make naming more than correct pronunciation: it is recognition within a covenant belonging. Read against what you have written, the weariness of being misnamed may touch a deeper question of whether it is worth being known accurately at all. Scripture does not make the wound trivial. It places identity first in the God who knows the name, claims the person, and redeems what fear threatens to diminish. Your grandmother’s word for the long ago also lingers here: the One who formed Israel is not forgetful across time.

What changes when being known by the LORD is held beside the places where your name is overlooked?
