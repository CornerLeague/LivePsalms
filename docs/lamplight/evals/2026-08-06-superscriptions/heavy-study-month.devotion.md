Priya — the psalm’s final word does not erase the plea; it gives the unsettled middle a place to stand.

Psalms 27:14

Wait patiently for the LORD; be strong and courageous. Wait patiently for the LORD!

The passage turns on repetition: “Wait patiently for the LORD” is both command and final line, after the psalm has moved from confidence into pleading. Read against your question, waiting is not presented as a detour from knowing the LORD, nor as arrival postponed into meaninglessness. It is the posture left when the singer has named fear, sought the LORD’s face, and cannot yet narrate a resolution. The strength and courage named here are therefore not triumphal. They belong inside dependence—remaining before the LORD rather than treating His house as a brief refuge. Psalms 27:14 lets the unresolved hinge of the psalm remain visible, while refusing to make that hinge its conclusion.

What changes if the final waiting is part of dwelling before the LORD, rather than the opposite of arrival?
