## Reply

Hebrews 11:1 gives less a dictionary definition than a description of faith in action. Jamieson reads it as “sure confidence”: faith treats God’s promised future as substantial now and regards unseen realities as trustworthy because God has spoken. The lexicon’s *pistis* likewise includes persuasion, conviction, reliance, and constancy. Thus faith is not optimism or the power to produce a desired outcome; it is durable reliance upon the faithful God. (Heb 11:1, 11:6)

The examples then unfold that description:

- **Faith understands** what cannot be directly observed: creation came by God’s command. (Heb 11:3)
- **Faith acts** before sight supplies certainty: Noah builds; Abraham leaves without knowing the destination. (Heb 11:7–8)
- **Faith judges the Promiser faithful** when circumstances argue otherwise, as with Sarah and Abraham. (Heb 11:11–12, 11:17–19)
- **Faith chooses by an unseen horizon**: Moses values God’s reward above Egypt’s visible wealth. (Heb 11:24–27)
- **Faith endures without immediate fulfillment**: the faithful confess themselves exiles and welcome the promise “from afar.” (Heb 11:13–16)
