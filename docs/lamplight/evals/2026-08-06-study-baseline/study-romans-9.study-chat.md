## Reply

Romans 9 clearly teaches that God’s electing purpose precedes human birth and works: Jacob was chosen “before the twins were born or had done anything good or bad,” so that the purpose would rest “not by works but by Him who calls” (rom 9:11–12). Paul also says mercy does not depend on human desire or effort (rom 9:16).

The contested question is **what that election encompasses**. One reading sees Jacob and Esau as examples of God choosing particular individuals for salvation. Another notes that Paul is tracing the covenant line—Isaac rather than Ishmael, Jacob rather than Esau—and that his quotation comes from Malachi’s address concerning Jacob/Israel and Esau/Edom. On that reading, the immediate emphasis is God’s freedom in directing redemptive history, not yet a complete statement about each man’s eternal destiny (rom 9:6–13; mal 1:2).

The chapter nevertheless moves from historical roles to people called as “vessels of mercy,” from both Jews and Gentiles (rom 9:22–26). Yet it closes by describing righteousness as received through faith and Israel’s stumbling as pursuit “as if it were by works” (rom 9:30–33).

So the careful answer is: **Romans 9 certainly teaches sovereign, pre-birth election; whether it teaches here that specific individuals are unconditionally chosen for eternal salvation is disputed among orthodox Christians.** The chapter holds divine mercy and the真实?—
