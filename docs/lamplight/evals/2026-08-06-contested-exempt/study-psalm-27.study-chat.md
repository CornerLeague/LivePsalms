## Reply

David’s wish is best read as a desire for **an entire life lived in the LORD’s presence**, not merely permanent residence inside a building. Psalm 27:4 unfolds the thought through three expressions: dwelling in the LORD’s house, gazing upon his beauty, and seeking him in his temple. Together they describe sustained communion, worship, and attentive dependence on God (Psalm 27:4).

The “house of the LORD” in David’s setting refers to Israel’s sanctuary—the place where God made his presence known among his covenant people. Yet David’s emphasis falls less on architecture than on access. His “one thing” is not first the removal of enemies, although enemies surround the psalm; it is the LORD himself. Even the lexicon’s sense of “seek” includes searching after God in worship or prayer. David wants every threatened day to be oriented around God’s face and character (Psalm 27:4, 8).

“To gaze on the beauty of the LORD” does not suggest seeing God physically. In the movement of the psalm, his “beauty” is encountered in who he proves to be: light, salvation, stronghold, shelter, and faithful receiver of the forsaken (Psalm 27:1, 5, 10). The sanctuary is therefore pictured both as a place of contemplation and as refuge. David longs to know the LORD so deeply that danger no longer determines the center of his life.

“All the days of my life” gives this desire its scope. He is not asking for a brief religious experience in a crisis, but for lifelong fellowship. The later prayer, “Your face, O LORD, I will seek,” makes the inward meaning explicit: dwelling with God means continually turning toward him (Psalm 27:8).
