# doubt-season · devotion

_Sustained honest doubt. Tests that it is not resolved on the reader's behalf._

Tom — when prayer seems to meet only a ceiling, the harder question may be why the ache to be heard remains.

Psalms 13:1

For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever? How long will You hide Your face from me?

Psalms 13:1 does not step around the strangeness of silence; it gives that strangeness words addressed to the LORD. The psalm’s opening turns on repetition—“How long?”—not as a solved argument, but as a refusal to make absence the final fact. Read against what you have written, the longing itself is part of the tension: you can name doubt aloud, yet still go among the singers and keep directing the question toward God. David’s complaint is not presented as polished certainty. It is prayer from inside felt abandonment. That makes room for your unsteady words without pretending the silence is simple.

What changes when the question “How long?” is allowed to remain a prayer rather than become a conclusion?
