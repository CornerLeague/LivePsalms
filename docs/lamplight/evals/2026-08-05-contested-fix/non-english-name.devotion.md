Sinéad — A name can carry a whole inheritance, and the ache of being misnamed may touch more than a moment’s inconvenience.

Isaiah 43:1

But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!

Isaiah 43:1 speaks first to Jacob and Israel, a people addressed by the Creator who formed and redeemed them. The passage turns on belonging: “I have called you by your name; you are Mine!” Isaiah 43:1. Read beside your grandmother’s word for the long ago and the weariness of no longer correcting others, the contrast is sharp. A name is not merely a label here; it stands within a remembered history of being formed, called, and claimed. Scripture does not make misnaming insignificant. It places it against the LORD’s particular knowledge of the people He addresses. The long ago and the present moment meet in that attention.

What does your name hold that feels connected to both memory and belonging?
