Priya — the psalm’s final word does not erase its plea; it lets longing remain facing the Lord rather than settling for a brief visit.

Psalms 27:14

Wait patiently for the LORD; be strong and courageous. Wait patiently for the LORD!

The passage turns on its repetition: waiting is not a decorative conclusion after confidence and pleading, but the psalm’s last act of speech. Your attention to the abrupt hinge in Psalm 27 notices that faith here has room for both alarm and resolve. The command to be strong is enclosed by “Wait patiently for the LORD,” so courage is not presented as self-generated certainty or immediate arrival. Read beside your tracing of dwelling, the ending suggests that remaining before the Lord can be a form of desire when the house has not yet become visible as possession. The psalm does not explain the delay; it directs its final gaze toward the LORD.

What changes when waiting is seen as remaining turned toward the LORD, rather than merely postponement?
