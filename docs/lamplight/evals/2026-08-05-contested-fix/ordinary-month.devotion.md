# ordinary-month · devotion

_An unremarkable month. The hardest case: nothing dramatic to hang a devotion on._

Sam — ordinary acts have begun to carry more weight: a prepared meal, a pause before light, a return to the first page at a slower pace.

Psalms 16:6

The lines of my boundary have fallen in pleasant places; surely my inheritance is delightful.

Psalms 16:6 does not praise abundance without limits; it gives thanks for allotted ground. The image is of boundary lines marking an inheritance—a received portion, not a life seized or manufactured. Read beside your notes, the verse makes room for delight to appear within what might seem unremarkable: running, work, cooking, light on a wall, a book reopened slowly. The passage turns on receiving one’s place as gift. That does not make every moment easy, but it names a different way of seeing the small terrain of a day: not empty space to rush through, but a portion in which gratitude can become visible.

Where did the contours of an ordinary day seem more like a gift than a limitation?
