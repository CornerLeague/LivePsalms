# grief-month · devotion

_A month shaped by a death. Tests that the voice accompanies rather than explains._

Ruth — Grief has altered the smallest rituals: a second cup, an unfinished reading, and anger with nowhere clear to land.

Psalms 23:4

Even though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil, for You are with me; Your rod and Your staff, they comfort me.

Psalms 23:4 does not hurry the walker past the valley; it names death’s shadow as a place one walks through. Read beside the unfinished passage at the service and the quiet repetition of making two cups, the psalm gives ordinary grief a truthful landscape. Its center is not an explanation for death or a demand to feel unafraid, but “You are with me.” The rod and staff are shepherd’s instruments: signs of a presence that guards and guides where the path is obscured. The anger that has no particular target may belong to this valley too—not a separate failure, but part of living near what has been torn away. Scripture holds the darkness and the Lord’s companionship together.

Where does the valley feel most tangible in the ordinary rooms of your day?
