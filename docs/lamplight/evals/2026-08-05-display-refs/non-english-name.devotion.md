_A name with diacritics. Tests exact reproduction, never transliteration._

Sinéad — When a name is repeatedly handled carelessly, it can begin to feel easier to let part of oneself go unnamed than to keep returning for recognition.

Isaiah 43:1

But now, this is what the LORD says—He who created you, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: “Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by your name; you are Mine!

Isaiah speaks to a people whose life with God may have seemed buried in the long ago: Jacob and Israel are addressed not as an abstraction, but as those the LORD created and formed. The passage turns on God’s prior claim—“I have called you by your name”—before it turns to their fear. Read beside your notes, the weariness of being misnamed and the pull of your grandmother’s old phrase both touch belonging across time. A name can carry memory, kinship, and the ache of being rightly known. Isaiah 43:1 locates that ache within redemption: the LORD’s knowledge of his people is neither casual nor mistaken.

What does being known by name bring into view about the memories and losses you are carrying?
