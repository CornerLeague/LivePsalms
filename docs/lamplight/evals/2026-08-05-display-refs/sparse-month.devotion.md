# sparse-month · devotion

_Two short notes in a whole month. Tests that thin input produces restraint rather than padding._

Dana — when a week leaves few words, even taking a seat at the back can become a way of naming weariness without explaining it.

Matthew 11:28

Come to Me, all you who are weary and burdened, and I will give you rest.

Matthew 11:28 does not require the weary to arrive with a polished account of what weighs on them. Jesus’ invitation begins with their condition: weary and burdened. Read beside your spare words and your place at the back of the service, the passage makes room for a faith that comes carrying little language. The rest Jesus names is not presented as a reward for having sorted the week out; it is given in coming to Him. The note’s movement toward worship, however unobtrusive, sits honestly inside that invitation. Christ meets more than articulate prayers; He receives the burdened person who simply comes.

What burden feels present even when you do not have many words for it?
