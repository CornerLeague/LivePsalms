Priya — the final word of a prayer can feel strange when the deepest desire is not movement, but remaining in the place where God is sought.

Psalms 27:14

Wait patiently for the LORD; be strong and courageous. Wait patiently for the LORD!

Your notes notice that Psalm 27 moves from confidence into plea, then ends not with arrival but with waiting. That ending matters: the psalm does not treat waiting as the opposite of faith, but as the place where courage is summoned before the LORD (Psalms 27:14). Read beside your attention to “dwell,” the tension becomes sharper. The desire to remain with God need not be satisfied by a resolved circumstance; the psalm’s last line keeps the seeker turned toward the LORD while the plea remains open (Psalms 27:14). Its repeated command gives waiting a weighty, active shape—not abandonment, but expectancy held before God.

What changes when waiting is viewed as remaining turned toward the LORD rather than delayed arrival?
