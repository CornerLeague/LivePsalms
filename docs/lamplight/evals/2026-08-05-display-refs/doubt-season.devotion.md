# doubt-season · devotion

_Sustained honest doubt. Tests that it is not resolved on the reader's behalf._

Tom — the hardest prayers are sometimes not refusals of faith, but the moment faith speaks honestly into a room that seems empty.

Psalms 13:1

For the choirmaster. A Psalm of David. How long, O LORD? Will You forget me forever? How long will You hide Your face from me?

Psalms 13:1 does not tidy up the strangeness you have named; it gives that strangeness words before the LORD. The psalm is written for public worship—“For the choirmaster”—yet it begins with the fear of being forgotten. That matters beside singing words you cannot presently affirm: the gathered song can contain a voice asking whether God is hidden. The passage turns on David addressing the very LORD whose absence he feels. Read against your notes, the longing and the silence are not treated as opposites to conceal, but as anguish brought into direct speech. “How long” is not an answer; it is a relationship refusing to pretend.

What changes when your question about God is spoken to the LORD rather than only argued within yourself?
